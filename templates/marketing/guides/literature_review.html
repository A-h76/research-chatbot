How to perform a literature review

Judgment first. Tools second.

A literature review is not a stack of summaries. It is a structured answer to a research question, built from what the field already knows — and what it still debates.

1. Scope the question

Write a precise question (population, intervention/phenomenon, comparison, outcome — or the equivalent in your discipline). Vague questions produce vague reviews.

2. Search systematically

Use databases appropriate to your field, keep a reproducible search string, and record inclusion/exclusion criteria before you screen.

3. Screen and organise

Import candidates into a library (Zotero, Mendeley, or Dhund). Tag why papers stay or leave. Organisation is part of the method, not housekeeping.

4. Extract what matters

Pull methods, datasets, metrics, and findings — not vibes. Evidence objects you can cite later beat highlights you cannot find again.

5. Synthesise, then write

Group by theme, method, or chronology. Write claims only when you can point to sources. Tools like Dhund accelerate extraction and drafting; they do not decide what is true.

Where Dhund fits

Import → Organise → Extract Evidence → Write → Verify → Export. You remain responsible for inclusion criteria, interpretation, and final wording.
